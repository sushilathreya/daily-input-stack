02 / The Principle

Give the picture a job in the sale.

Visual treatment is strongest when it makes the offer easier to see, understand, believe, or desire. The image should serve the promise, not compete with it.

One example

A meal-kit ad should show the finished dinner and the ease of preparing it if those are the reasons to buy, not an unrelated lifestyle scene that earns attention but leaves the offer vague.

Eric S. Raymond / for_you / 2026-11-27

A product fragment on turning capability into behavior

This fragment is useful because it names the behavioral question behind a new capability. The important test is not whether the feature is impressive, but whether it changes a repeated workflow enough for the user to adopt it.

Panic fatigue. I've been thinking about why the op against Jensen Huang failed, and now I wonder if we might be seeing the end of a major phase of American politics. Ever since the 1960s American politics has been arguably dominated by a series of panics. The population bomb. Global cooling. Global warming. AIDS. Acid rain. COVID. Peak oil. Swine flu. Mad cow disease. The coming mass famines. Saturday night specials. Satanic panic. Even, killer bees. And today, AI apocalypse. More could be added. Over and over again the same pattern. Fear-mongering. Media hype. Most importantly, political power grabs at various scales. "Disaster is coming! Surrender your liberty so we can keep you safe!" Skeptics stigmatized not with the language of science but with moral condemnation. Censorship deployed to silence dissenters. And then the failure of prediction, the apocalypse that never arrives...and the panic-mongers never, ever acknowledging that they were wrong. Never being held accountable for the damage they did. But after every cycle, the bureaucratic state is just a bit more smothering, keeping some of the reach it gained during the crisis. Everything gets more expensive. The quality of American Life degrades. Social trust weakens. Go through this enough times, and it is not surprising that people start developing panic fatigue - reflexively looking for the con behind the hype. They start asking "Where's the power grab this time? Who benefits?" This time, the EA/Doomer network tried to gin up a full scale panic about runaway superintelligence when it was really about making a regulatory moat for a handful of AI cloud providers, and suppressing open source AI to lock down the future. Huang was pushed out because he is a powerful voice against all that. And people noticed. It didn't take months, or even weeks. In less than 11 days, the time it took for Huang to be reinstated, the attempt to suppress open source became an everybody knows that everybody knows thing. And then the public case against Jensen Huang fell apart. Not the private complaint that his wife and his family legitimately has, but the rationale for his ouster. Dare I hope that, as a society, we are becoming skeptical enough not to be fooled again? To notice when we're being played? I think we need a name for this monster. "Witch hunt" doesn't quite cover it because that is a panic directed at marginalized individuals, rather than a power grab over the whole of society. "Moral panic" isn't quite right either, even though there is pretty much always an element of moral panic; it short-changes the political dimension. A robot friend suggests the following... Panic imperialism: The political exploitation of a real, exaggerated, or fabricated threat to legitimate expansions of institutional power and social control that are disproportionate to the actual requirements of addressing the threat. The definition is good. I'm not quite satisfied with the label. The robot also suggested "apocalyptic statism" which is in some ways better. The point is, to learn how to recognize when this playbook is being run, and jump on it before it builds a lot of momentum. I think AI itself may have helped. It's easier than it has ever been to fact-check panic hype, and people are doing it. Stay skeptical. Watch for the sleight of hand. We're in an information-rich, intelligence-rich environment now; they can't really fool you unless you let them. Don't let them.
