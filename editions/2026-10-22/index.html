Treat irrationality as evidence, not error.

In the opening pages of Chapter 1 of Rory Sutherland's Alchemy, reason becomes useful when it knows what it cannot explain. The first question is not why a choice looks irrational, but what human value the model has left out.

01 / Reason is a tool, not a complete picture

Sutherland's target is not clear thinking; it is the belief that one style of reasoning can account for every human decision.

As you read, separate a model's usefulness for one problem from the temptation to treat it as a description of the whole person.

02 / Irrational behaviour may contain the missing value

A choice that looks wasteful or inconsistent may be protecting reassurance, identity, status, convenience, or a social meaning the spreadsheet does not record.

The practical move is to ask what the behaviour accomplishes before asking how to eliminate it.

03 / Audit the model before correcting the person

When observed behaviour repeatedly defeats the official explanation, the discrepancy is evidence. It may reveal a variable the system has made invisible.

Choose one live example and write the stated reason, the observed behaviour, and the human motive or context the stated reason leaves out.

Close Reading Sequence

  1. Where does Sutherland distinguish useful reasoning from the overreach of treating every choice as rationally calculable?
  2. What kind of human value might a narrow model erase from the behaviour it is trying to explain?
  3. Choose one behaviour your team calls irrational. What does the behaviour achieve for the person who chooses it?
  4. What evidence would show that the model is incomplete rather than that the person is making a mistake?
  5. What is the smallest observation or experiment that could reveal the missing variable?

02 / The Principle

Treat the anomaly as a clue to the missing human value.

A tidy explanation can still be too thin. When people persistently act against the model, look for the emotional, social, contextual, or symbolic payoff the model has failed to measure.

One example

A team removes a human onboarding call because self-serve setup is faster. If serious buyers hesitate, the call may have been supplying reassurance and recognition rather than mere instruction; the efficient model priced the wrong thing.

Deliberate practice - 15 minutes

Pick one user behaviour your team labels irrational. Write the official explanation, the observed behaviour, and three human variables the explanation omits. End with one test that could distinguish a flawed model from a flawed choice.

03 / Field Notes

Four current signals about the human value hidden inside efficient systems.

Anthropic / 2026-10-15

Independent evaluation is becoming part of the product

What happened: Anthropic said it is giving METR, a nonprofit frontier-model evaluator, employee-like access to evaluate the safety of its systems and is discussing similar arrangements with other organizations. Why it matters: The company is admitting that internal confidence is not enough evidence for systems with unfamiliar failure modes. External access creates a route for discovering what the builder's own model of risk has missed. Watch: When a system makes consequential claims about safety or quality, ask who can inspect it with enough access and independence to find the failure the builder cannot see.

Marketing Dive / 2026-10-21

Target makes the store part of the holiday story

What happened: Marketing Dive reports that Target's 2026 holiday campaign turns gift-giving into a fictional academy, extends the idea through creators and social video, and carries the visual system into stores. Why it matters: The campaign treats the physical store as a marketing medium and a place where the story can be experienced, not just a last-mile fulfillment box. Meaning and guidance become part of the shopping value alongside price. Watch: When a campaign promises help with a human problem, carry the promise into the environment where the choice is actually made instead of leaving it inside the ad.

Anthropic / 2026-10-18

Anthropic is making public benefit an ownership mechanism

What happened: Anthropic announced that, subject to stockholder approval, 5% of its shares will be committed to a new Anthropic Public Benefit Fund, with the board able to add 2% for every 50% increase in company value above a baseline. Why it matters: Mission is being expressed through a structural claim about who benefits from growth, not only through messaging. The design attempts to preserve a public-purpose payoff that a conventional value-maximizing model might leave outside the ledger. Watch: When a company claims a social purpose, look for the ownership, governance, or allocation mechanism that makes the promise costly to abandon.

OpenAI / 2026-10-21

Ads are being designed for conversations that have not reached a conclusion

What happened: OpenAI expanded ChatGPT Ads to 26 additional countries, bringing availability to 89 countries, and described ads appearing while people explore, compare, and decide what to buy in a multi-turn conversation. Why it matters: The commercial unit is becoming a developing intention rather than a single query. A useful ad must respect the uncertainty and changing constraints in the decision, instead of treating every mention as a purchase signal. Watch: Separate exploration from commitment in your funnel; the right intervention may be the one that helps a person clarify the problem rather than forcing an immediate conversion.

05 / The Practice

Let strange behaviour improve the model.

Before optimizing away an irrational-looking choice, ask what human value it is defending and whether your explanation has earned the right to dismiss it.
