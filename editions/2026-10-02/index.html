Chapter Reader

Read this chapter as a warning against importing mature-market planning into an emerging market. Mark where Christensen replaces prediction with discovery, where customers reveal the product's real use, and where an early failure teaches the team what the market is not. Then write one discovery brief: target customer, job to be done, first use case, learning experiment, and evidence that would change the product.

01 / Emerging markets cannot be analyzed in advance

A market that does not yet exist cannot be described with the same confidence as an established one. The relevant customers, applications, and dimensions of performance are still being formed.

The strategy therefore has to be a plan for learning and discovery. It cannot be a detailed execution plan built on assumptions that the market itself has not validated.

02 / Customers and suppliers discover together

In a disruptive market, customers often cannot specify the product they need because they have not yet experienced the new possibility. Suppliers learn what to build by placing imperfect solutions in real contexts and observing what becomes useful.

The early product is not only a delivery mechanism. It is an instrument for discovering the customer's job, the valued outcome, and the market's actual boundaries.

03 / Early applications reveal the market

The first viable use may not resemble the large market eventually imagined. An application that looks small or peripheral can reveal the performance tradeoff, customer, or workflow that makes the technology meaningful.

Treat those applications as evidence about the market rather than forcing them to satisfy a forecast made for a different one.

04 / Learning changes the product definition

Because the market is discovered through use, product strategy must remain responsive to what customers do with the technology. The team learns not only whether demand exists, but what the product is actually for.

This is why disruptive innovation requires patience with ambiguity: premature certainty can close off the very observations that would define the opportunity.

05 / Build a discovery loop before a scale plan

The practical assignment is to name the customer, job, first application, and experiment that can produce the next piece of market knowledge. The goal is not to predict the final market; it is to make the next uncertainty testable.

Stop with one emerging-market discovery brief: current assumption, smallest useful experiment, evidence to watch, and the product or customer definition that would change if the evidence disagrees.

02 / The Principle

Make the next market fact discoverable.

When customers and applications are still forming, strategy should organize learning rather than simulate certainty. Use small experiments to discover what the market values before scaling the plan.

Example

A software company exploring an AI workflow should begin with one narrow team and one recurring job, observe where the tool changes behavior, and let those observations define the product before forecasting a category-sized market.

Deliberate practice - 15 minutes

Choose one emerging opportunity. Write the current customer and use-case assumption, the smallest experiment that can test it, the behavior you will observe, and the product decision that follows from each possible result.

03 / Field Notes

Four current signals on discovering the market through real customer behavior.

Retail Dive / 2026-10-01

Meta brings an AI agent into the small-business stack

What happened: Meta launched Muse for Small Business, an AI agent that can work across business software including Instagram, Facebook, Shopify, Stripe, QuickBooks, Asana, Box, Canva, and Zoom, with human approval available before purchases or publishing. Why it matters: The product is entering the market through an existing operating context rather than asking small businesses to adopt another isolated assistant. The real discovery question is which cross-tool tasks owners repeatedly trust the agent to handle. Watch: Watch which approved workflows become habitual, and where businesses still require a human handoff before the product definition can expand.

Retail Dive / 2026-10-01

AI referrals are becoming a measurable shopping channel

What happened: Adobe expects AI traffic to U.S. retail sites to more than double this holiday season. In its survey, AI-referred shoppers added items to carts at a 32% higher rate, while 43% generated more revenue per visit than non-AI traffic. Why it matters: The emerging market is not simply AI search; it is a new discovery and referral layer in which brands must be legible to both the shopper and the shopper's assistant. The observed behavior is more useful than a forecast about eventual channel size. Watch: Watch whether higher cart activity persists through completed purchases, returns, and repeat visits once more autonomous shopping agents enter the path.

Marketing Dive / 2026-09-30

Kroger links creative choices to conversion before media spend

What happened: Kroger, Vidmob, and MMA Global used predictive creative scoring across more than 1,900 assets and reported 81% accuracy in forecasting e-commerce conversion; aligned creative averaged a four-times improvement in conversion rates. Why it matters: This is a market-discovery loop for marketing itself: creative attributes become hypotheses, live sales become the evidence, and the next media allocation changes accordingly. The advantage is not merely using AI, but building a proprietary feedback loop around real outcomes. Watch: Watch whether the predictive signal transfers across audiences and formats, or remains useful only inside the specific campaign context from which it was learned.

TechCrunch / 2026-10-01

ChatGPT tests shopping through try-on and saved intent

What happened: OpenAI launched virtual try-on for clothing and accessories plus Favorites, allowing shoppers to upload a selfie or product image, visualize an item, and save products in a Library for later. Why it matters: The product is probing two concrete behaviors—confidence before purchase and deferred intent—rather than claiming that conversational shopping is already a finished category. Those behaviors can reveal whether visual assistance earns a place in the buying journey. Watch: Watch whether try-on increases qualified product exploration and eventual conversion, or whether the novelty ends before shoppers make a purchase.
