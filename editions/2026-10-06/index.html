Use genre as a promise, then make the promise your own.

In Chapter 14 of Story, Robert McKee treats genre as a contract with the audience: a familiar pattern of values, conflicts, and emotional movement that gives a story its expectations. The writer's task is to satisfy that contract while finding a distinctive way through it.

Apprenticeship Note

Genre is not a shelf label added after the work is made. It is a promise about the kind of conflict and feeling the audience will receive; learn the promise well enough to keep it, then make your choices distinctive inside it.

01 / Genre is an audience contract

Genre gives the audience a set of expectations about the kind of world, conflict, value change, and emotional experience the story will provide.

Those expectations are not a limitation to evade. They are part of the communication system that lets a new story become legible before every detail is known.

02 / Choose the genre's central value

A genre is more than a collection of surface devices. Its identity comes from the values it puts under pressure and the kind of change the audience is invited to feel.

Study what moves from positive to negative, or from negative to positive, and the conflicts that make that movement meaningful.

03 / Familiarity creates room for attention

The audience can spend attention on character, situation, and surprise because genre conventions establish a working map. Without that map, novelty may become confusion rather than interest.

The writer earns freedom by understanding the expectation that a choice is about to confirm, complicate, or overturn.

04 / Originality lives inside the frame

A story becomes distinctive through its specific world, characters, pressures, and sequence of choices, not through refusing every convention at once.

Use the genre promise as a constraint that sharpens invention: keep the emotional contract clear while making the route to its fulfillment particular to this story.

05 / Turn the reading into a genre brief

Name the genre, the central value it puts at stake, the audience expectation it creates, and the specific variation your story will introduce.

Stop with one brief that distinguishes a meaningful variation from a decorative twist. The test is whether the audience can recognize the promise and still feel the work has something of its own to say.

02 / The Principle

Use conventions to make originality legible.

A familiar form gives the audience a map for attention. Learn the values and emotional movement the form promises, then make the work distinctive through the particular world, pressure, and choices you place inside it.

Example

A founder story may use the familiar arc of a difficult problem overcome, but its distinctiveness can come from the unusual customer, constraint, or moral tradeoff that changes what success means.

Deliberate practice - 15 minutes

Choose one story, campaign, or product narrative. Name its genre promise, the value it puts at stake, the emotional movement the audience expects, and one specific choice that makes the work recognizably its own.

TikTok turns discovery into an explicit commerce promise

What happened: TikTok announced Buy Direct in-app checkout, Shopping Assistant for conversational product guidance, Agentic Leads, a TikTok Ad Network reaching nearly 400,000 apps, and Smart+ tools that combine brand, AI-generated, and creator content in one approval workflow. Why it matters: The platform is making its promise legible across the whole journey: discover something culturally relevant, understand it in context, and act without leaving the surface. The convention is familiar, but the product is trying to make the route from story to transaction feel native. Watch: Watch whether the added automation preserves a distinctive creative point of view, or turns every brand into interchangeable optimized inventory.

A product fragment on turning capability into behavior

This fragment is useful because it names the behavioral question behind a new capability. The important test is not whether the feature is impressive, but whether it changes a repeated workflow enough for the user to adopt it.

Yes. Switzerland has cheese. Congratulations, everyone. We know. The slightly more interesting fact is that this is Indian cheese that has actually competed against 1000s of cheeses from around the world and won international awards. @eleftheriaindia, a Mumbai cheesemaker, had a cheese rank #4 in the world at the 2023 World Cheese Awards, competing against 4,500+ cheeses from 43 countries. It has since picked up more international recognition, including Best Indian Cheese at the 2025 World Cheese Awards in Switzerland. So maybe, just maybe, the point isn’t “India has better cheese than Switzerland.” The point is: India makes really bloody good cheese. And if a PM meeting the Swiss President is an opportunity to put an Indian food business w genuine global credentials in front of Switzerland, why wouldn’t you do it? We are so used to complaining that India doesn’t market itself, doesn’t build brands, doesn’t export enough cultural soft power etc. And then when someone actually puts an Indian product on the diplomatic table, we’re like: “BUT SWITZERLAND HAS CHEESE 😭” Please.
