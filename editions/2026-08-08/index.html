The Apprentice's Desk

Growth is a team sport before it is a metric.

Sean Ellis and Morgan Brown argue that sustainable growth is not the output of a clever channel or a marketing department. It is a cross-functional learning system with clear ownership, executive cover and one meaningful problem at a time.

Canon: Sean Ellis and Morgan Brown, Hacking GrowthStudy: 25-35 minPractice: one growth-team charter

Apprenticeship Note

A growth metric can create activity without creating learning. Give a small cross-functional team authority over a single customer problem, then ask it to produce evidence before asking it to produce scale.

01 / The Canon

Ellis and Brown: Growth needs authority to cross the lines on the org chart.

Read the six-page run from "Executive Sponsorship Required" through "The Reporting Structures for Teams" in Chapter One of Hacking Growth. It makes a simple claim: a growth team is not an experiment factory bolted onto marketing. It is a small, cross-functional unit with enough sponsorship to test across product, data, design and distribution.

Chapter Reader

Read once for the organizational constraint: what prevents a team from acting on a good experiment? Read again for the design choice: who owns the outcome, which functions must be represented, and how will conflict be resolved when the work crosses their boundaries?

01 / Give the work a real sponsor

Ellis and Brown are explicit that growth cannot be a side project. A team that must negotiate every product, design, data and channel decision after the fact will learn slowly, whatever its dashboard says. The growth lead needs a clear reporting line and senior backing strong enough to cut through ordinary departmental ownership.

That is not a plea for executive theatre. It is a practical precondition for running a coherent loop: form a hypothesis, make a change, measure it, and decide what to do next. If the team cannot make or secure the change, the loop is broken before the data arrives.

02 / Put the full customer path in the room

The book treats growth as a shared problem because the customer does not experience a company in functional silos. An acquisition promise, an onboarding screen, a product interaction and a retention message are one journey to the person using the product. The team therefore needs product, engineering, design, data and marketing perspectives close enough to work as one unit.

Cross-functional does not mean a standing committee. It means each specialist has a defined contribution to a single growth priority and can make decisions at the speed of the experiment. A team that merely collects updates from functions has coordination; it does not yet have shared learning.

03 / Choose the structure that protects learning

The authors distinguish product-led teams, which report into product leadership, from independent teams with a broader mandate. Neither model is universally right. The useful question is whether the structure gives the team enough proximity to the customer problem and enough freedom to test across the funnel.

Start with the narrowest mission that can generate a credible win: improve activation for one audience, repair a retention moment, or learn one acquisition channel. A visible, well-instrumented result earns the trust and operating room required for a wider mandate.

Close Reading Sequence

  1. What decision rights does a growth team need in order to complete one experiment loop without a handoff?
  2. Which customer problem currently crosses the most functions in your organization? Who can convene the people needed to learn from it?
  3. Is your current growth work product-led, independent, or neither? What does that structure make easy and what does it make slow?
  4. What is the smallest mission that could produce a credible win within four weeks?
  5. Where is executive sponsorship necessary to resolve a real trade-off, rather than simply to approve a dashboard?

02 / The Principle

The unit of growth is the learning loop, not the department.

If a customer problem crosses product, design, data and distribution, the team learning about it must cross those same boundaries. Name an owner, give the group one decision-sized mission, and judge it by the evidence it produces—not by the number of ideas it collects.

One example

A subscription product sees trial starts rising while week-two retention falls. Marketing wants more qualified traffic, product wants a new feature and design wants a cleaner onboarding flow. Instead of three separate roadmaps, a small growth team owns one question: what action in the first session predicts a return? It runs one onboarding test, instruments the result and takes the evidence back to all three functions.

Deliberate practice - 15 minutes

Write a one-page charter for one growth problem: name the customer moment, a single owner, the five functions that must contribute, one leading behavior, one guardrail, and the exact decision the team can make without asking for another meeting.

03 / Field Notes

Three signals that growth gets faster when the customer loop, not the silo, owns the work.

Marketing Dive / 2026-08-03

Domino’s pays diners to beta test its relaunched app, website

What happened: Domino’s is offering eligible digital-order customers a $5 coupon while it promotes and gathers feedback around its relaunched website and app, linking the product experience, offer and customer response in one programme. Why it matters: This is more useful than treating a relaunch as a finished product handoff. The campaign puts distribution, use and feedback into the same loop—the kind of cross-functional surface a growth team can learn from quickly. Watch: Whether the feedback and repeat-order behavior identify a specific ordering friction that the next product and messaging iteration can resolve.

Retail Dive / 2026-08-06

Home Depot reshuffles leadership to accelerate tech innovation

What happened: Home Depot moved store, supply-chain and Pro-product technology teams under its new CTO while tying the change to simpler core offerings, interconnected shopping and professional-customer growth. Why it matters: The reorganization treats customer experience as a system rather than a store, supply-chain or app metric. That is the organizational premise in today’s reading: growth work moves at the speed of the slowest boundary it must cross. Watch: Whether the shared leadership model yields visibly shorter cycles between operational insight and a better DIY or Pro customer experience.

TechCrunch / 2026-08-07

After Rippling blew millions on AI in months, it built an employee ROI tool

What happened: Rippling built an internal tool to make AI spend and employee-level return more visible after moving quickly on AI tools, turning a broad technology budget into a question of adoption and value. Why it matters: A growth team needs a measure close enough to the behavior it is trying to improve. The lesson is not to install more measurement; it is to create shared visibility that lets finance, operators and product teams test the same value hypothesis. Watch: Whether the tool changes allocation decisions and workflows, rather than becoming a retrospective cost dashboard.
