02 / The Principle

Design for the experience the number cannot capture.

A commercially important improvement may be small in physical terms but large in perception. Find the threshold where the change becomes felt, understood, and worth choosing.

One example

A hotel may spend less on a technically superior material and more on a small change in lighting, arrival, or tactile comfort that makes the room feel markedly more welcoming.

Deliberate practice - 15 minutes

Pick one product or service and describe the smallest physical change that could produce the largest useful change in how it feels. Name the context and the behaviour you expect to change.

03 / Field Notes

Four current signals about turning small perceptual shifts into value people can feel.

Marketing Dive / 2026-11-17

Holiday brands are using human-made detail as a felt difference

What happened: Marketing Dive reports that holiday marketers are balancing AI-enabled tactics with creator partnerships, deals, and human-made creative. Levi's used stop-motion claymation that required four hours of filming per second, while E.l.f. used stop-motion to make individuality tangible in its first holiday campaign. Why it matters: The physical production difference is small relative to the media system around it, but it becomes a legible cue for care, craft, and distinctiveness. The perceptual shift is the point: viewers can feel that the work was made differently before they can explain why. Watch: Whether the extra craft improves brand association and attention, or is remembered only as an executional novelty after the holiday campaign ends.

Retail Dive / 2026-11-17

Holiday shopping is stretching across more moments, not one sale day

What happened: Retail Dive reports that consumers began shopping early to manage budgets, while nearly 30% plan to do most holiday shopping in early to mid-December, up from about 20% last year. BCG also finds generative AI becoming a tool of choice for finding and comparing products, with more than half of consumers using it in 2026. Why it matters: The calendar is not just a schedule change; it changes the felt meaning of a promotion. Early discovery, comparison, and reassurance can matter as much as the discount itself because the shopper is managing uncertainty over time. Watch: Whether retailers design different cues for early planning, comparison, and last-minute urgency, instead of repeating the same sale message across the whole season.

Meta Newsroom / 2026-11-17

Meta makes agentic help concrete through local connectors

What happened: Meta made Muse available in Australia with local connectors across creativity, shopping, property, fitness, and entertainment, including Canva, Domain, Kogan, MECCA, Officeworks, and Ticketek. Users choose access and Muse checks with them before sensitive actions such as sending email or making a purchase. Why it matters: The product becomes more understandable when an abstract agent is attached to familiar services and a visible permission boundary. The connector and approval details are small design choices, but they turn autonomy into something that can feel useful without feeling uncontrolled. Watch: Whether local, everyday connectors create repeat behavior faster than a broad promise about an all-purpose personal agent.

Google / 2026-11-18

Google makes generated worlds easier to feel and revisit

What happened: Google rolled out Genie 4 to eligible Google AI Ultra subscribers in Project Genie, adding real-time natural-language editing of environments, native audio, longer sessions, and a community gallery for sharing creations. Why it matters: The new capability is easier to value because it crosses a perceptual threshold: generated worlds can now be changed, heard, and revisited as places rather than treated as one-off outputs. A gallery also makes the experience social and repeatable. Watch: Whether longer sessions and sharing create a durable creative habit, or whether the novelty fades before Project Genie owns a clear job.

05 / The Practice

Make the invisible value easier to feel.

The aim is not to ignore engineering or measurement. It is to notice where human perception multiplies, discounts, or redirects what the specification says should matter.
