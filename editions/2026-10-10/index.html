01 / Start with the monetization funnel

Monetization differs by business model, but the first discipline is the same: map the customer journey and identify where value can become revenue.

Study the places where customers encounter the offer, choose a plan, complete a purchase, renew, upgrade, or create inventory that can be sold to advertisers.

02 / Find the pinch points

Revenue leaks often appear between intention and completion: a product page, pricing screen, payment step, upgrade path, or ad placement can lose value through friction.

The growth team should use data to locate the specific step where potential earnings are lost, then formulate an experiment around that observed weakness.

03 / Segment customers by revenue

Average revenue can hide meaningful differences between cohorts. Compare customers by plan, spend, engagement, acquisition source, device, geography, and behavior to discover who pays and why.

A useful cohort is not just a description; it gives the team a hypothesis about which experience, offer, or message could increase revenue without treating every customer alike.

04 / Learn what customers value

Analytics can show where revenue changes, while surveys and customer conversations can help explain which benefits, features, plans, or payment options make the offer worth choosing.

The goal is to connect a customer's actual need to an experiment in packaging, pricing, feature emphasis, or conversion—not to guess at willingness to pay from a single metric.

05 / Turn the leak into one experiment

The practical output is a focused monetization hypothesis: a target cohort, a value or revenue problem, a change that could address it, and a measurable signal of improvement.

Stop with one monetization experiment brief. Make the intervention small enough to learn from and specific enough to show whether the customer received more value or merely faced more pressure to pay.

02 / The Principle

Capture more of the value you already create.

Sustainable monetization comes from understanding the customer journey, locating revenue leaks, and testing ways to help the right customers receive and pay for more value.

Example

A workflow product might discover that small teams reach the pricing page but abandon when they cannot see how the plan supports their first shared project. A focused experiment could show that workflow in the plan comparison, then measure qualified upgrades rather than page clicks alone.

Deliberate practice - 15 minutes

Choose one product and write a monetization experiment brief: target cohort, value to increase, current revenue leak, smallest intervention, observation window, and behavior that would count as success.

OpenAI / 2026-10-07

OpenAI turns the answer surface into an interactive product

What happened: OpenAI announced GPT-6 with Intelligent UI, which can compose responses from text, visuals, tappable buttons, forms, charts, and interactive experiences, including tools created in the conversation itself. Why it matters: The monetization question moves from whether a model can answer to whether the surrounding interface helps someone complete a valuable task. The product is trying to capture more of the value by reducing the handoff between explanation, decision, and action. Watch: Watch which interactive responses become repeat workflows rather than impressive one-off demonstrations, and whether the added interface measurably improves completion or retention.

October deal events are pulling higher-value purchases forward

What happened: Adobe data cited by Retail Dive put U.S. online sales during Amazon's two-day Prime Big Deal Days at $9.86 billion, with mobile accounting for 52% of sales and the share of the most expensive goods up 21% versus the year-to-date average. Why it matters: Discounting did not merely create more transactions; it changed the mix and timing of demand. The useful monetization lens is cohort and context: which shoppers trade up, which essentials move, and whether an October purchase steals from November or expands the season. Watch: Watch post-event repeat rates, November demand, and whether mobile-first urgency produces durable customer value or just a temporary revenue spike.

Omaha Steaks replaces vague quality language with proof

What happened: Omaha Steaks' new No Bull platform makes sourcing, aging, and hand-cutting explicit across TV, audio, paid social, and influencer partnerships. Why it matters: A brand claim becomes more monetizable when customers can understand what the claim means and why it is credible. The experiment is not simply a new slogan; it is a clearer value proposition attached to evidence and a faster path to purchase. TechCrunch / 2026-10-06

Agent commerce is exposing a new checkout pinch point

What happened: TechCrunch reported that consumer AI agents can be blocked by retailer anti-bot systems or human-verification flows, even as Meta, Walmart, Stripe, and other partners work on an open standard for agent-to-agent commerce. Why it matters: The value chain now has a new leak between customer intent and completed purchase: permission to act. A retailer that welcomes a verified personal agent can preserve the transaction, while an opaque block may lose both the agent and the human behind it. Watch: Watch which sites expose reliable agent permissions and identity signals, and whether conversion improves when the shopper can delegate research and checkout without being treated as a bot.

Patrick Collison / for_you / 2026-10-09

Personal agents may make product quality easier to discover

Patrick Collison argues that personal agents could reduce the advantage of short-attention demand routing and make product quality, fit, and evidence more important in purchase decisions.

Assorted things I've been mulling over regarding agents in the economy: • Companies are in some sense the original superintelligence. It's often the case that they can use their amortized cognitive surplus to advance their interests against those of certain consumers: relative to the optimization capacity of companies, consumers can be inattentive, underinformed, irrational, etc. • In corporate strategy, people have long debated the relative importance of distribution and product quality. Perhaps you've made something better, but does anyone know about it? Won't people just continue to buy the ACME Corp Mousetraps? Personal agents will, I think, act as a kind of structural subsidy for product quality. • Many internet companies have monetized demand routing, which is in turn often predicated on short attention spans. How will the nature and value of this routing change in a world of personal agents? I don't know the answer, but I think that the effects might be pretty big.
