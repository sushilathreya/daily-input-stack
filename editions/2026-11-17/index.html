01 / Find the mental frame

Ries and Trout treat positioning as the battle for a place in the mind, not a complete description of reality.

Mark the simple association that makes the subject easier to retrieve than a list of generic virtues would.

02 / Narrow the claim

A crowded category punishes broad claims because many competitors can repeat them.

Notice what the chosen frame leaves out and why that sacrifice makes the position more memorable.

03 / Make the association credible

A mental position becomes useful when the name, story, and evidence reinforce the same expectation.

Underline the details that turn a slogan into a believable reason to choose.

04 / Audit one position

Choose one brand, city, or product whose communication currently tries to stand for too many things.

Rewrite its position as one association, one contrast, and one proof point.

02 / The Principle

Make one association do the work of many claims.

A strong position gives people a simple mental route to the choice: one frame, a clear contrast, and enough evidence to make the association stick.

One example

A destination does not call itself innovative, authentic, affordable, and premium at once. It chooses the one travel expectation it can own and lets the itinerary, imagery, and proof reinforce it.

Deliberate practice - 15 minutes

Take one brand or place and write three lines: the association to own, the competing frame to reject, and the proof that should make the association credible.

03 / Field Notes

Four current signals on making one association easier to retrieve, use, and trust.

Google Registry / 2026-11-10

Google turns the web address itself into a category cue

What happened: Google Registry launched the .eat, .fly, and .here top-level domains, with public availability at a base annual price beginning November 17, 2026. The examples are designed to signal a restaurant, travel service, or place to get something done before a visitor clicks. Why it matters: This is positioning at the address layer: the name narrows the expected job before the product has to explain itself. The association is useful because it removes interpretive work from the first step of discovery. Watch: Audit the first five seconds of your own customer journey: does the name make the intended use easier to retrieve, or does the visitor have to decode a generic promise?

Meta Newsroom / 2026-11-16

Meta is separating close connection from the larger social feed

What happened: Meta introduced Direct, a standalone messaging app from Instagram that is being tested in Türkiye and Thailand. It is positioned around one-to-one and group conversations, with creative tools such as Draw, Notes, Stickers, Voice Effects, and themes, plus protections built in from the start. Why it matters: The product is not asking users to remember every Instagram job. It claims a narrower frame—close messaging—and makes the features reinforce that frame. A focused association can make a familiar capability feel like a distinct choice. Watch: When a product expands, watch whether the new surface owns a clear job or merely duplicates the parent brand with more features.

Shopify / 2026-11-13

Shopify makes discoverability part of the commerce promise

What happened: Shopify said AI-driven traffic to its stores grew 2.6x year over year and AI-referred orders grew 3.3x, while arguing that connected agents can help shoppers find products beyond the first page of results. It also reported that about 75% of AI-referred purchases came from outside the top 100 product categories. Why it matters: The commerce position is shifting from being easy to find on one shelf to being legible across many agents. A brand's association has to survive the retailer, search surface, and recommendation layer that interprets the catalog. Watch: Audit the product data an agent sees: is the use case, difference, and proof point explicit enough to earn a recommendation outside the obvious category shortlist?

Meta Newsroom / 2026-11-15

Singapore small businesses say AI needs a clearer operating frame

What happened: Meta reported that 94% of surveyed Singapore small-business owners said they need more guidance to use AI, alongside a finding that more than three quarters said AI makes them more productive. Why it matters: The signal distinguishes favorable belief from a usable position. Productivity is a broad benefit; guidance is the missing frame that helps a business know where the tool belongs, what to do first, and how to judge the result. Watch: If an AI product claims to help businesses, look for the narrow workflow, first successful use, and proof metric that turn general optimism into repeat behavior.
