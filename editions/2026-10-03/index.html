01 / Retention is the test of real value

Acquisition can create a first visit and activation can create an initial aha moment, but retention asks whether the product remains useful after novelty and onboarding have passed.

The growth team should therefore treat return behavior as evidence about the product's continuing value, not as a communications problem to solve only with more reminders.

02 / Cohorts show where users leave

Retention becomes actionable when users are grouped by signup date, acquisition source, behavior, or other meaningful differences. Cohorts reveal whether abandonment is concentrated in a particular path or customer type.

The point is not to admire a single retention percentage. It is to find the segment and moment where an experiment could change the curve.

03 / Repeated value needs a product loop

Features, notifications, and rewards can strengthen the habit of returning when they connect to the value users already seek. Personalization helps when it makes the next useful action easier to find.

Retention weakens when teams add features faster than customers can understand them. More capability is not automatically more value; feature bloat can hide the product's core promise.

04 / Resurrect the users who already know the value

A lapsed user is different from a stranger: they have already experienced the product's potential. A resurrection flow can remind them of the aha moment and invite a return through a relevant message or offer.

That work should follow the larger retention diagnosis. Winning back inactive users matters, but the first priority is understanding why new users fail to become active and repeated users.

05 / Turn the drop-off into one experiment

The practical discipline is to connect one cohort, one abandonment point, and one repeated-value hypothesis. Test the smallest product or message change that could move users back toward useful activity.

Stop with one retention experiment brief: name the cohort, the observed drop-off, the value to reinforce, the intervention, and the behavior that would count as evidence.

02 / The Principle

Measure the return, then earn it.

Durable growth comes from repeated value. Use cohorts to find where people leave, strengthen the product loop that brings them back, and test changes against observed behavior rather than relying on more acquisition alone.

Example

A project tool should identify whether teams return to review decisions, assign work, or resolve blockers, then improve the one repeated workflow that predicts continued use instead of adding a wider feature menu.

Deliberate practice - 15 minutes

Choose one product and write a retention experiment brief: target cohort, current drop-off, repeated value to strengthen, smallest intervention, observation window, and behavior that would count as success.

03 / Field Notes

Four current signals on how products earn return visits through useful loops, measurable creative, and better context.

Retail Dive / 2026-10-01

AI-referred shoppers are arriving with stronger purchase intent

What happened: Retail Dive reported that Adobe expects AI traffic to U.S. retail sites to rise 130% year over year this holiday season. Adobe found AI-referred visitors added items to carts at a 32% higher rate, while AI users generated 43% more revenue per visit than non-AI traffic in its complementary survey. Why it matters: Discovery is becoming part of the retention and conversion loop before a shopper reaches a brand site. The product has to be easy for both the human and the assistant to understand, compare, and trust; a first visit is less valuable if the experience cannot support the next decision. Watch: Whether brands redesign product information and post-purchase experiences for AI-mediated discovery without losing the human reasons customers return.

Retail Dive / 2026-10-01

Meta extends its AI agent into the small-business operating loop

What happened: Retail Dive reported that Meta launched Muse for Small Business, an agent that can draw on Instagram and Facebook business accounts and connect with tools including Shopify, Stripe, QuickBooks, Asana, Box, and Canva. The product is free up to a token limit, with paid plans from $20 to $100 per month, and businesses can require human approval before purchases or publishing. Why it matters: Adoption is being framed around repeated operational value rather than a one-off answer. Integrations, approvals, and trusted records give a small business a reason to return because the agent is attached to work already in motion. Watch: Whether the strongest AI products win by becoming dependable workflow loops with permission boundaries, rather than by accumulating disconnected capabilities.

Marketing Dive / 2026-09-30

Kroger is testing creative quality before media spend is committed

What happened: Marketing Dive reported that Kroger, Vidmob, and MMA Global used more than 1,900 Kroger video and image assets to build predictive creative scoring. The study said the approach forecast e-commerce conversion with 81% accuracy, while creative aligned with the recommendations produced an average four-times improvement in conversion rates. Why it matters: This moves experimentation closer to the decision point that creates the result. Instead of waiting for a campaign to spend and then diagnosing the drop-off, the team is trying to identify which creative is worth giving a larger opportunity to earn attention and action. Watch: Whether predictive scores continue to correlate with incremental sales across channels and audiences, rather than becoming another proxy metric teams optimize in isolation.

Marketing Dive / 2026-10-02

Beer brands are cutting support for work that cannot prove incremental sales

What happened: Marketing Dive reported that Boston Beer increased advertising, promotion, and selling expenses 16.4% year over year in Q2 while revenue fell 3.3%, and that the company is cutting lower-performing advertising. Its founder said the company has removed support until advertising can prove it improves incremental sales. Why it matters: Retention thinking applies to budgets as well as products: attention and spend should return to the work that creates observable value. The lesson is not to demand certainty from every experiment, but to define the behavior and commercial signal that earns the next round of investment. Watch: Whether more brand teams connect creative decisions to incremental business outcomes instead of treating reach or activity as evidence of durable growth.
