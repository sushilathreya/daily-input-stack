01 / The writer turns instinct into design

An idea is only a beginning. The writer gives it force by choosing what the audience will know, want, fear, and discover next.

As you read, distinguish the initial intuition from the decisions that make it dramatic rather than merely interesting.

02 / Every choice should create movement

A scene, character action, or structural turn earns its place when it changes the situation and narrows or reshapes what can happen next.

Look for choices that create consequence. If nothing becomes more difficult, more valuable, or newly possible, the material may be description rather than story.

03 / Constraints sharpen invention

Creative discipline is not the enemy of originality. Limits force the writer to find the particular action, image, or sequence that carries the idea with precision.

Apply the same test to a brand or product narrative: what constraint makes the promise more specific instead of merely more elaborate?

04 / Revise for necessity

Revision is the search for causal strength. Remove a choice and ask whether the following event still has a reason to occur; if it does, the earlier move may not be doing enough work.

Mark the point where the reading turns craft into a repeatable diagnostic rather than a mysterious talent.

05 / Audit one live piece of work

Choose a page, pitch, campaign, or product story. Name its central desire, the pressure opposing it, and the choice that changes the audience's understanding.

Stop with one revision that makes the next move necessary. Do not add explanation where a stronger choice would create the meaning.

02 / The Principle

Make each creative choice carry consequence.

A story becomes persuasive when its choices alter the field of possibility. Give the audience a desire, a pressure, and a decision whose result makes the next move feel earned.

One example

Instead of saying that a product helps a team collaborate, show a project lead choosing to expose a blocked dependency before a launch review; the choice creates risk, reveals the value, and makes the team's next action necessary.

Deliberate practice - 15 minutes

Take one page, pitch, or campaign sequence. List its current desire, pressure, choice, and consequence. Remove one decorative move and replace it with a decision that changes what the audience expects next.

03 / Field Notes

What the field is teaching now.

Meta / 2026-10-13

Personal agents are being sold as follow-through, not just answers

What happened: Meta introduced Muse Wonder and Muse Wonder Full for personal and professional agentic tasks, emphasizing Active Memory and Active Focus so an agent can retain relevant context, try alternate approaches, recover from errors, and ask before important actions. Why it matters: The product story is moving from fluent response to accountable follow-through. The value is not that an agent can act once, but that it can keep a goal intact across difficulty without silently taking control. Watch: For an agentic promise, specify the task that must be completed, the recovery behavior when it fails, and the checkpoint that keeps the user in charge.

Adweek / 2026-10-20

Away turned a viral failure into a product proof point

What happened: Adweek reports that Away made DJ John Summit its Chief Destruction Officer after a viral video of him burning a Rimowa suitcase following repeated breakage; the partnership puts Away luggage through deliberately extreme tests. Why it matters: The brand did not merely borrow attention from a viral moment. It created a new scene in which the product promise—durability—could be tested in public, giving the audience a reason to keep watching and a concrete claim to remember. Watch: Look for the product or service proof that can turn a borrowed cultural moment into a designed experience with a clear consequence.

Associated Press / 2026-10-15

Holiday spending is growing, but inflation is doing part of the work

What happened: The Associated Press reports that the National Retail Federation forecasts $1.01 trillion in U.S. holiday spending for November and December, up 4.2% year over year, while economists expect higher prices to account for about half of the increase and higher-income shoppers to drive much of the spending. Why it matters: A larger market number can hide a thinner customer reality. The useful story is not simply that demand is healthy, but which shoppers can absorb price increases and which are editing the occasion around value. Watch: Separate nominal growth from real demand before choosing a holiday promise; specify the customer, the pressure, and the trade-off the offer removes.

Google / 2026-10-15

Google is turning the shopping cart into a decision assistant

What happened: Google launched Universal Cart in the U.S. across Search and the Gemini app, combining products from multiple retailers, price-drop tracking, promo-code discovery, payment-method suggestions, and checkout or handoff to the merchant. Why it matters: Commerce is becoming a sequence of small decisions delegated to the interface: collect, compare, watch price, and choose how to pay. The product earns trust by making each next move legible and useful rather than hiding the transaction behind automation. Watch: When designing a commerce assistant, map the user's decision points and show where automation saves effort, where it makes a recommendation, and where the customer must still choose.
