Apprenticeship Note

Character becomes legible at the moment a person must choose between what they say they value and what they are willing to do to get what they want.

01 / The Canon

McKee: character is the choice that pressure makes visible.

Read Chapter 11, "Character," from Robert McKee's Story, on PDF pages 257-281. McKee treats character as the revealed truth of a person in action: pressure forces choices, and those choices expose the difference between characterization and the deeper design of character.

Chapter Reader

Read for the gap between presentation and behavior. Mark what a character claims, what they pursue, what pressure removes from their options, and what choice finally reveals. Then apply the same test to a customer, brand, or protagonist whose stated identity is not yet supported by action.

01 / Character is revealed, not announced

McKee separates characterization—the observable surface of habits, appearance, speech, and social role—from character, the deeper nature revealed when a person must act under pressure.

A description can tell the audience who someone appears to be. Only a consequential choice shows what that person will protect, sacrifice, betray, or become.

02 / Desire gives behavior a direction

Character becomes readable through pursuit. A person who wants nothing has no meaningful line of action, while a specific desire gives each decision a direction and makes resistance consequential.

For a commercial narrative, name the protagonist's object of desire before adding personality language. The desired change is what lets the audience judge every later choice.

03 / Pressure exposes the contradiction

The useful dramatic test is the gap between what a person says and what they do when the easy option disappears. Pressure brings competing values into conflict and makes the contradiction visible.

Do not solve the contradiction with a backstory label. Put the character where two real goods, loyalties, or fears cannot all be preserved, then watch the choice produce meaning.

04 / Choice changes the audience's understanding

A strong character action does more than move the plot. It changes the audience's interpretation of everything that came before and creates a new set of expectations for what can happen next.

That is why a brand's values are most credible when a costly decision proves them. The claim matters less than the constraint under which the organization is willing to honor it.

05 / Build the person through action, not decoration

The chapter's practical discipline is to make every trait earn its place in the causal design. A detail belongs when it affects desire, conflict, choice, or consequence; otherwise it is decoration around an untested person.

Stop with the chapter's pressure test in hand: identify the choice that would make the audience revise its view of the character, and build the narrative toward that choice.

Close Reading Sequence

  1. How does McKee distinguish characterization from character, and why can a detailed surface portrait still fail to reveal the person?
  2. Choose one consequential choice in the chapter's examples. What does the character say they want, what pressure changes the options, and what does the action reveal?
  3. Where does McKee use contradiction to deepen a character rather than make the character merely inconsistent?
  4. What desire gives the character's actions a direction, and how would the scene change if that desire were removed?
  5. Map one customer or brand narrative: stated identity, real desire, pressure, irreversible choice, and the new audience expectation created by that choice.

02 / The Principle

Make the value claim costly enough to become believable.

A character, brand, or product becomes credible when pressure forces a choice that reveals what it actually values. Design the constraint, then let the action carry the claim.

One example

A service says it protects customer focus. When a high-value account asks for a distracting custom exception, the company declines the feature, explains the boundary, and offers the supported workflow that preserves the promise.

Deliberate practice - 15 minutes

Choose one protagonist, customer, or brand you are writing about. List the identity it claims, the desire driving it, the pressure that would expose a contradiction, and one costly choice that would make the underlying value visible.

What happened: OpenAI described consumer and enterprise adoption as mutually reinforcing, reporting more than one billion weekly active users, 2.5 million businesses, and higher daily message volume plus broader task experimentation among individual-plan users six months after signup. Why it matters: The credibility claim is being carried by behavior over time. Watch: Whether usage depth continues to grow as access expands, or whether the next constraint becomes trust, workflow integration, and the cost of turning more possible tasks into valuable ones.

Ram Ahluwalia CFA, Lumida / following / 2026-09-15

A product fragment on turning capability into behavior

This fragment is useful because it names the behavioral question behind a new capability. The important test is not whether the feature is impressive, but whether it changes a repeated workflow enough for the user to adopt it.

‘Attention Is All You Need’ is the whitepaper that unlocked the AI cycle. Much of what followed has been duct tape. I don’t mean that as an insult. Duct tape built half the modern world. Consider two of the most important techniques driving AI forward today. First: chain of reasoning. Instead of asking the model to take one clean shot at an answer, we give it more inference time to decompose the problem, explore alternatives, check its work, and try again. Step. Check. Revise. Second: loops. One model creates. Another evaluates. The first revises. The cycle continues until the output survives the critic. At Meta, Muse is the public example. Meta’s AI chief noted that putting an eval LLM around a creator LLM, run the loop repeatedly, produce work comparable to that of a hundred engineers. Impressive. Also: It’s more MacGyver duct tape. That is what frontier AI increasingly looks like. So why do the models keep improving? Scale. More data. More parameters. More training compute. More inference compute. Better chips. Better post-training. The breakthroughs have lagged the expectations. Another reason AI leaders are saying ‘time to slow down’ is not the catastrophe risk which is nonsense. It is that expectations are being lowered and managed. Root Cause: The brightest minds in the AI research world - Ilya, Murati, Dean are all raising gobs of capital instead of doing research that sparked the initial breakthru.
