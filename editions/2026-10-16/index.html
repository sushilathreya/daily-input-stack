Apprenticeship Note

A market can become vulnerable when the incumbent keeps improving along dimensions customers no longer value. Find the performance customers can actually use, then look for the simpler offer hidden beneath the surplus.

01 / Improvement can outrun use

A product can become more capable than its target customer requires. When improvement continues along the incumbent's preferred dimension, additional performance may stop creating additional value.

Study the difference between what a technology can do and what a customer has the time, skill, or willingness to use.

02 / Oversupply changes the basis of competition

Once performance is sufficient, customers may care more about convenience, speed, simplicity, affordability, or accessibility than another increment of technical excellence.

Notice how the criteria that win in an established market can become less important when the product has already crossed the customer's threshold of usefulness.

03 / Disruption starts where the incumbent is satisfied

A disruptive offer often begins in a market the incumbent considers unattractive because it is smaller, lower-margin, or less demanding on the incumbent's traditional performance metric.

Ask why the incumbent's apparent strength can make the new offer easy to dismiss even while customers find its different tradeoff compelling.

04 / Follow the unmet job

The opportunity is not simply to make a weaker product. It is to solve a job for customers who are over-served by the dominant product or excluded by its cost and complexity.

Identify the job that becomes visible when you stop treating the incumbent's performance measure as the only definition of value.

05 / Write one oversupply diagnosis

02 / The Principle

Compete on the need the incumbent has stopped seeing.

Disruption becomes possible when improvement outruns usefulness. Look for customers who are over-served by the dominant offer, then design around the convenience, price, or simplicity they still cannot get.

Example

A professional editing suite may keep adding controls that expert users value while overwhelming small teams that only need fast, reliable video output. A simpler tool can win by reducing setup time and cost, even if it offers fewer advanced features.

Deliberate practice - 15 minutes

Choose one product category and write five lines: over-served dimension, excluded customer, unmet job, simpler tradeoff, and the first signal that the tradeoff is becoming valuable.

03 / Field Notes

Four current signals on where simpler, more usable offers are finding room beneath incumbent performance.

TechCrunch / 2026-10-14

Figma gives agents a simpler design-language brief

What happened: Figma introduced Specs, a Markdown-based editor where teams can define design language and connect design and code libraries so agents can follow the system more predictably. Why it matters: The important shift is from adding more generative power to reducing the effort required to get a useful, on-brand result. The simpler interface is a governed brief that makes capability usable for more teams. Watch: Watch whether design-language files become the practical bridge between powerful agents and teams that cannot afford to manage every output manually.

TechCrunch / 2026-10-15

Google turns scattered shopping research into one cart

What happened: Google launched Universal Cart in the U.S., letting people collect products found across Search and Gemini, track price and availability, and continue toward checkout across retailers. Why it matters: The product does not win by adding another storefront. It removes the friction of remembering, comparing, and restarting a purchase journey, making convenience the basis of value after product discovery is already abundant. Watch: Watch whether saved intent and price alerts create a durable shopping habit, or whether shoppers still abandon the journey before an agent-mediated checkout.

Marketing Dive / 2026-10-15

Consumer brands spend harder while tech platforms pull back

What happened: A WARC Media report found major consumer-goods marketers increased advertising intensity from 2023 to 2025 while technology companies reduced ad spend relative to revenue as their platforms captured more attention. Why it matters: The market is separating between companies that own a direct attention channel and brands that must pay to reach customers. That creates room for sharper, more efficient propositions rather than simply louder media plans. Watch: Watch whether retail media and tighter category-specific promises help consumer brands regain efficient reach, especially with value-conscious shoppers.

Retail Dive / 2026-10-15

Bealls makes the loyalty layer easier to reach

What happened: Off-price retailer Bealls announced a mobile app alongside its For the Haul of It campaign, linking store discovery, customer acquisition, loyalty, and lifetime value as it expands beyond 650 locations. Why it matters: The app is a lower-friction entry point into an existing treasure-hunt proposition. It helps an expanding physical retailer make discovery and return behavior easier without pretending the customer needs a more complex shopping system. Watch: Watch whether the app increases repeat visits and loyalty participation, or merely adds another channel without improving the reason customers return to stores.

Rex Salisbury / for_you / 2026-10-15

Selling is the fastest test of whether the market cares

This is useful because it collapses a comforting research loop into the market's actual test: will anyone take action? It belongs beside the canon as a reminder that evidence improves a diagnosis only when it changes what you do next.

Ramp is nearing 10% market share. That is bad. It sounds awesome..."Look at how much TPV we are doing $275 billion annualized TPV (cards and AP) in the $2.23 trillion corporate card market" You don't want to build a company with "a lot of market share." You want to build a huge company. Once you cross "10% market share"...you can only grow ~10x from there! Look at the other corporate card issuers. Nielsen US Commercial Card Report (2024) implies Ramp, growing as fast as they are, could soon become the second biggest! - AMEX has 24% ....Ramp? - JPM has 10% - BofA has 9% - US Bank has 5% Going from 0->10% is hard...but you can do it without upsetting anyone. You can grow and everyone else can grow too. Going from 10-20% is even harder. Growth becomes zero sum. wonder why Mercury, Ramp and Airwallex are fighting on X...they are getting bigger and growth is increasingly zero sum! Growing from 10->20% starts to reshape the entire industry I said "this is bad". The truth is "this is exciting". Ramp's mission is not "be the biggest corporate card company"....the mission is "save companies time and money." Ramp has reached the end of the beginning of their story. Eric says as much "Chapter 1 is over. Chapter 2 is just starting" (paraphrased). If you are investing in Ramp today, you are not investing in "the biggest corporate card company". You are investing in a company that has bigger mission. They need to keep growing market share, but more importantly, they need to grow their product lineup. JPM morgan is nearly a $1 trillion company. Corporate cards are just a small small part of what they do. If Ramp wants to be a $1 trillion company...well...get back work. The whole Ramp team is well aware of the above. Why do you think the focus on shipping new product so fast? why do you think they are launching AI products? Ramp is also in some ways a story of much of fintech at this point. Robinhood is also at ~10% market share for example. The whole industry of plucky upstarts w/ <10% market share is now starting to change the entire ecosystem. I love this! I want the entire industry to change. It is just starting. Fintech is still early. I look forward to seeing what Ramp (and everyone else) ships next! I'm excited to watch them (and everyone else) continue to grow market share, but I'm even more excited to see what their products might become. What if corporate cards turn out to be just a small part of what Ramp does in the future? If that happens, I'm here for it! *re: market share. Ramps TPV includes all forms of spend, not just card spend. so it's hard to get an exact market share percentage. "is nearing 10%" is not precise, but directionally correct. 05 / The Practice

Find the customer the performance curve leaves behind.

When the incumbent keeps improving past the point of usefulness, change the question from 'How do we add performance?' to 'Which customer needs a different basis of value?'
