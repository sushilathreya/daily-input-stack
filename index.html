01 / Find the threshold

Satisficing is not surrendering standards; it is choosing a threshold that makes a decision usable under real constraints.

Mark the point where the chapter moves from abstract best-possible performance to a choice that works well enough in context.

02 / The Principle

Optimize the decision that matters, not the metric that is easiest to measure.

A useful choice sets a credible threshold, accounts for the value the model cannot see, and spends further effort only when the improvement will change the outcome.

One example

A team choosing project software does not need the platform with the longest feature list. It may choose the tool that clears its adoption threshold, preserves the workflow people actually use, and leaves attention for the work itself.

Deliberate practice - 15 minutes

Pick one decision you are over-optimizing. Write the outcome that would make it good enough, the unmeasured value your current scorecard misses, and the signal that tells you to stop searching.

03 / Field Notes

Four current signals on choosing useful thresholds while the world gets noisier.

Retail Dive / 2026-11-18

Holiday shoppers want AI help, but not an unbounded handoff

What happened: A Retail Dive report on a survey of more than 1,000 U.S. adults found shoppers expect AI to save about $192 on holiday purchases and reduce shopping time, but 41% had abandoned a cart when a chatbot could not answer basic shipping or inventory questions. Only 18% would let an AI agent make a purchase above $500. Why it matters: The useful threshold is not full automation. Shoppers want AI to handle comparison and search while the retailer remains accountable for live facts, clear limits, and the final consequential choice. Watch: Watch whether commerce agents make the handoff from recommendation to human review explicit, especially when inventory, delivery, refunds, or higher prices are involved.

Retail Dive / 2026-11-18

Home improvement retailers turn digital convenience into growth

What happened: Home Depot reported its sixth consecutive quarter of double-digit online comparable growth, with digital sales up about 13% year over year; Lowe's online sales rose 17.4%. The retailers cited improvements including AI-enabled search, broader assortment, faster checkout and delivery, and marketplace range. Why it matters: The winning metric is not the presence of AI. It is whether a set of small improvements clears the customer's practical threshold: finding the right item, trusting availability, and getting it with less friction. Watch: Watch which operational details sustain digital growth after the novelty of AI-enabled search fades, and whether speed and assortment improve the whole job rather than one click.

Marketing Dive / 2026-11-17

Brands are using human-made craft as a counter-signal to AI sameness

What happened: Marketing Dive reports that holiday marketers are balancing AI-assisted discovery with resistance to AI-generated marketing materials. Levi's used stop-motion claymation, requiring four hours of filming per second, while E.l.f. used stop-motion to reinforce its individuality platform. Why it matters: In a market where output is abundant, visible effort can become part of the product's meaning. The labor is valuable only when it connects to a brand promise customers can recognize, not when it is merely expensive production. Watch: Watch whether handcrafted execution improves memory, preference, or loyalty beyond campaign admiration, and whether the signal remains credible when every brand borrows the same nostalgia cue.

OpenAI / 2026-11-18

OpenAI measures progress at a jagged frontier rather than one score

What happened: OpenAI published Research acceleration: The jagged frontier, describing how coding agents are accelerating AI research while performance remains uneven across tasks. The framing focuses on where agents help, where they fail, and what that unevenness implies for recursive self-improvement. Why it matters: A capability label is too coarse for a real decision. Teams need a task-level threshold: where the agent is dependable enough to delegate, where verification remains mandatory, and where a headline improvement does not change the workflow. Watch: Watch whether evaluations become specific enough to guide delegation and review, rather than turning uneven capability into a single reassuring benchmark number.

A product fragment on turning capability into behavior

This fragment is useful because it names the behavioral question behind a new capability. The important test is not whether the feature is impressive, but whether it changes a repeated workflow enough for the user to adopt it.

"grandpa, what was life like before super intelligence?" well kids back in 2026 something absolutely extraoridinary was happening. humanity was on the verge of creating intelligence greater than anything that had ever existed before. one of the leading companies in this revolution decided to go public at the time. the venture capitalists were all eagerly awaiting liquidity. i knew the company rather well. i understood most of the technology, the business model, the landscape, & the implications of what they were building. & what those would mean for society. i read the s1 cover to cover. i believed the company would fundamentally alter the trajectory of the human civilization. "so you invested, grandpa?" no. you see kids, the valuation was a tad too rich for me. while i liked the business, i did not like the multiple. "what happened next?" well, they went on to create recursive super intelligence that cured every disease, solved nuclear fusion, colonized three planets, discovered new laws of physics, and even figured out how to make french ppl happy. the stock went up 40,000%. 05 / The Practice

Make the good-enough choice consciously.

The aim is not lower standards. It is to recognize when a more precise answer would be less useful than a decision that fits the whole situation.
