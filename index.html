Apprenticeship Note

A brand earns a place in a customer's story when it helps the customer become someone new. Describe the transformation the offer can credibly support, then make the brand's role in that change clear.

01 / The Canon

Let the customer become someone new with your help.

Read Chapter 11, "People Want Your Brand to Participate in Their Transformation," from Donald Miller's Building a StoryBrand. Miller moves from describing a customer's desired outcome to showing how a brand can become a meaningful participant in the change the customer wants to make.

Chapter Reader

Read this chapter as a study of transformation-led positioning. Mark how Miller distinguishes a product's function from the identity or life change the customer is pursuing, how a brand can participate without taking the customer's role, and where the promise must remain credible. Then audit one offer: name the customer before-state, desired after-state, and the specific role the brand can play in helping the change occur. Stop at the end of Chapter 11.

01 / Name the transformation

Start with the person the customer wants to become, not only with the object or service being purchased.

Separate the functional job from the identity, confidence, status, belonging, or capability the customer hopes the purchase will support.

02 / Give the brand a useful role

A brand can participate in transformation by providing tools, guidance, proof, structure, or a community that helps the customer move forward.

Notice the difference between helping the customer act and making the brand the hero of the story.

03 / Make identity legible

Transformation becomes persuasive when the customer can recognize the new identity or life state the offer supports.

Study how language, examples, rituals, and evidence can make that future feel both desirable and attainable rather than merely aspirational.

04 / Audit one transformation

Choose one offer and write the customer's before-state, desired after-state, and the obstacle between them.

Then state the brand's smallest credible contribution: what it gives the customer to help make the transformation real, and what remains the customer's responsibility.

02 / The Principle

Position the brand as a credible partner in change.

Customers often buy toward an identity or life state, not only toward a feature. Make the desired transformation visible, then show exactly how the offer helps the customer move toward it.

One example

A writing course does not stop at "learn better copy." It shows the customer becoming someone who can explain an offer clearly, publish consistently, and earn trust through useful work, while naming the practice the customer must do.

Deliberate practice - 15 minutes

Choose one offer. Write the customer's before-state, desired identity, obstacle, and after-state. Then write one sentence explaining the brand's concrete contribution and one sentence naming the transformation it cannot promise on the customer's behalf.

03 / Field Notes

What the field is teaching now.

Marketing Dive / 2026-11-05

Fresh Step turns AI production speed into a relevance system

What happened: Fresh Step and agency RECE produced three 15-second spots in 30 days at roughly one-tenth the cost of traditional production. The brand used AI to prototype concepts visually, make the strategy and creative work observable midstream, and design modular ads that can be refined from market response. Why it matters: The meaningful transformation is operational: AI changes a campaign from a single expensive bet into a repeatable learning system. The customer-facing promise still has to be credible, but the brand can now test and improve the way it expresses that promise much faster. Watch: Watch whether the modular creative produces better brand and sales signals, not merely more variants or cheaper output.

Retail Dive / 2026-11-03

Holiday shoppers want AI assistance without surrendering the choice

What happened: Retail Dive reports that one-third of consumers are very or extremely willing to use generative AI for holiday recommendations, while only 13% currently use it for inspiration. Shoppers value price comparison and discovery, but many still want to review the purchase themselves because gifting carries a personal, human meaning. Why it matters: A useful customer transformation is not always full automation. Here the desired state is a more confident, less effortful shopper who still owns the final decision; products that remove friction without erasing agency may earn more trust. Watch: Watch where users accept delegation—research, comparison, or checkout—and whether the product makes review and control conspicuous.

Google / 2026-11-04

Google moves agentic shopping from discovery toward checkout

What happened: Google expanded UCP-powered direct checkout to Canadian shoppers in AI Mode and Gemini, including eligible lululemon, Shopify, Shoppers Drug Mart, and Simons offers. Retailers remain seller of record, while Google says saved Wallet details and future loyalty support should reduce checkout friction. Why it matters: The brand relationship is moving into an AI-mediated moment of intent. Retailers can participate in the transformation from scattered research to completed purchase, but they must preserve recognition, trust, and a clear role for the merchant inside the new interface. Watch: Watch whether customers remember and prefer the retailer after an AI surface completes the transaction, not just whether conversion rises.

Meta Newsroom / 2026-11-05

WhatsApp makes an event product useful beyond its own network

What happened: WhatsApp added an Events Home, customized invitations, RSVP management, and shareable invites that people without WhatsApp can open and respond to without downloading the app. The rollout keeps phone numbers private and preserves end-to-end encryption for messages and calls. Why it matters: The product participates in a social transformation—turning a group chat into a real-world gathering—by reducing the boundary between members and non-members. The invitation link is a small but important role: it helps the user include others without demanding identity conversion first. Watch: Watch whether the open invite expands attendance while the privacy boundary remains legible to both members and guests.

OpenAI / 2026-11-05

OpenAI frames stronger cyber capability around authorized defense

What happened: OpenAI introduced GPT-6 Cyber and described work with security teams to find vulnerabilities, validate impact, and develop fixes. The release pairs higher capability with authorization monitoring, critical-infrastructure safeguards, and a stated focus on keeping model actions within scope. Why it matters: Capability alone is not the customer transformation. For security teams, the credible promise is moving from scattered clues to verified fixes while retaining control over what the system may touch; guardrails are part of the product's role, not an appendix to it. Watch: Watch how customers verify model findings and enforce scope in long-running workflows before treating autonomous discovery as dependable.
