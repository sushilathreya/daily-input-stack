01 / Start with natural buying behaviour

Sharp does not deny that people repeat purchases. He asks whether repeat buying should be treated as evidence of a special psychological relationship or as a predictable feature of how category buying normally works.

Mark the point where a description of behaviour becomes a claim about why the brand grows.

02 / Study divided loyalty

Buyers often divide their purchases across several brands. This makes the category, not a single brand, the right context for interpreting loyalty metrics.

Notice what shared buying says about the limits of treating each brand's customers as a separate tribe.

03 / Test passion against penetration

A small group of enthusiastic buyers can be vivid and strategically flattering without being the main source of growth. Compare advocacy, frequency, and commitment claims with the broader pattern of who buys the brand.

Ask whether the evidence points to a stronger relationship or simply to a brand with more buyers and more buying occasions.

02 / The Principle

Describe loyalty before you prescribe it.

Repeat buying is a real pattern, but it does not automatically prove a special customer tribe or a growth strategy. Use category context and penetration evidence to separate what buyers do from the story a brand tells about why they do it.

One example

A coffee brand sees that its regular buyers purchase it often and launches a plan to make those buyers even more devoted. A better audit asks how many category buyers it reaches, how many also buy competitors, and whether easier mental and physical availability would create more growth.

03 / Field Notes

Four current signals on turning recognition, reach, and usefulness into repeat buying.

Google / 2026-11-12

Google makes task difficulty part of the product choice

What happened: Google said Gemini 4 Hydrogen is rolling out globally in Search with better handling of complex, multi-part questions, simultaneous queries, structured answers, and more visual content. Why it matters: The feature is not only a model upgrade; it gives Search a clearer job in moments when a user needs synthesis rather than a list of links. A repeat-use pattern can grow from being useful at a recognizable task, not from a vague promise of intelligence. Watch: Watch whether people return to Hydrogen for complex decisions because the output is easier to act on, or whether the richer interface merely creates novelty without a durable use case.

Anthropic / 2026-11-09

Anthropic turns model range into a routing habit

What happened: Anthropic introduced Claude Fable 5.2 and Mythos 5.2 for demanding technical work, while recommending Opus 5.5 for most daily coding and knowledge tasks and positioning the other models as choices for different scopes and risks. Why it matters: The company is teaching customers a buying and usage heuristic: start with the daily driver, then move up or down as the job changes. That makes product-family recognition operational rather than merely aspirational. Watch: Watch whether users can reliably tell which model fits which job, and whether the routing rule creates repeated adoption across the family instead of concentrating all demand on the most impressive name.

Marketing Dive / 2026-11-12

Fetch embeds brands inside a reason to keep watching

What happened: Fetch is launching a six-part holiday microseries with one brand partner tied to each episode, while viewers can earn points toward gift cards by watching and shopping through the app. Why it matters: This is a test of whether brands can earn repeated attention by becoming part of a useful entertainment-and-reward loop, rather than asking a small fan group to care more intensely. The relevant growth question is reach and repeat participation across the category of viewers. Watch: Watch completion, repeat viewing, and partner-level reach separately; strong engagement from a narrow rewards cohort would not by itself prove broad brand growth or memory.

Retail Dive / 2026-11-12

Convenience becomes repeat behavior only after a successful first use

What happened: Retail Dive reported that one-fifth of consumers expect to use buy now, pay later more often during the 2026 holiday season, while the survey also found that familiarity and convenience are stronger drivers of repeat use for tap-to-pay, digital wallets, and peer-to-peer apps than novelty alone. Why it matters: The finding is a useful warning against calling early enthusiasm loyalty. Repeat behavior follows a successful, familiar first transaction and a clear job to be done; the growth question is whether the service can widen adoption beyond the people already inclined to use it. Watch: Watch first-transaction success, repeat use, and category reach separately; a favorable attitude among current users is not the same as broad penetration.

A startup wins when the new physics force a new story

Founder succession is an open problem. Successful founders are themselves odd ducks who surround themselves with people who complement their strengths and weaknesses. By definition, the people who surround a founder are very different from the founder. But when a founder leaves, we promote the most successful subordinate into an organizational spot which they, almost by definition, don’t fit. Replacing a visionary founder CEO with a CFO or COO is comically stupid. Yet it is considered the best practice. 05 / The Practice

Turn the loyalty story into a measurable question.

A disciplined growth diagnosis names the buying pattern, places it in the category, and tests whether reach and availability—not a flattering customer myth—better explain the opportunity.
