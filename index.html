Apprenticeship Note

When an offer is not moving, inspect the surrounding context before adding more features. Meaning, choice, and expectation can change value without changing the object itself.

01 / Find the value around the object

Start with the underlying object or service, then separate it from the meaning and expectation surrounding it.

Mark where the chapter shows context changing perceived value without changing the physical substance.

02 / Study the choice architecture

Small changes in sequence, comparison, permission, or available options can change what people choose.

Notice which constraint or cue makes the preferred action easier to understand or easier to take.

03 / Treat irrationality as a clue

An apparently irrational response may be evidence that the person is optimizing for meaning, reassurance, status, or control.

Ask what human job becomes visible when the narrow functional model is widened.

04 / Run one context audit

Choose one offer and write the object, the current frame, the behaviour you want, and the context change that could support it.

Keep the underlying promise honest; change the surrounding meaning or choice only where it creates real usefulness.

02 / The Principle

Change the frame before you change the product.

Perceived value is produced by the object and the context around it. Before adding features or cutting price, inspect the frame, comparison, expectation, and choice architecture that determine what the offer means.

One example

A workshop can stop selling an hour of instruction and frame the same session as a decision clinic that leaves the participant with one resolved bottleneck and a next action.

Deliberate practice - 15 minutes

Choose one offer. Write the physical or functional object, the current context, the human meaning the buyer is seeking, and one honest change in framing, sequence, comparison, or choice that could increase perceived value.

03 / Field Notes

Four current signals about making value legible through context, trust, and choice.

Marketing Dive / 2026-11-19

Coca-Cola retreats from divisive AI holiday creative

What happened: Coca-Cola launched its 2026 holiday campaign with TV, digital, social, experiential, packaging, and partnerships, while moving away from the generative-AI approach used in its recent holiday pushes after those ads proved divisive with consumers. Why it matters: The underlying product has not changed; the context and production signal around it has. The brand is treating perceived warmth, tradition, and recognisable human participation as part of the value of the seasonal offer. Watch: Whether the human-made creative produces stronger brand association and attention than the technically efficient work it replaces, rather than only a short-lived backlash reversal.

Meta Newsroom / 2026-11-23

Meta shows open-weight models entering medical workflows

What happened: Meta described researchers using its open-weight models for medical training in virtual reality, epilepsy research, and analysis of rare-disease microscopy images. Why it matters: The value is framed through a concrete institutional context rather than model novelty: the same general capability becomes meaningful when attached to a difficult, bounded research gap and a human-led workflow. Watch: Whether these early-stage projects produce validated clinical or training outcomes, rather than only a compelling context around the models.

TechCrunch / 2026-11-23

Supertake turns a market opinion into an investable product

What happened: TechCrunch reports that Supertake raised $20 million to let users describe a market view in natural language, refine it with an AI agent, and turn the result into an investable portfolio through connected brokerages. Why it matters: The framing changes the object from ‘choose stocks’ to ‘express a view and see it operationalised.’ That context can make a complex action feel approachable, while the real product burden becomes trust, limits, and consequences. Watch: Whether users treat the conversational framing as a useful decision aid or as permission to outsource high-stakes judgment without understanding the portfolio.

05 / The Practice

Design the meaning around the thing.

Do not confuse contextual value with decoration. Find the surrounding cue, comparison, or choice that helps the person experience the real promise more clearly.
