Apprenticeship Note

A product does not need every possible advantage in the buyer's mind. It needs one clear association that makes the choice easier and the alternative less relevant.

01 / Find the position

Start by naming the simple association the product wants to occupy in the buyer's mind.

Notice which details support that association and which details would only add noise.

02 / Study the comparison

A position becomes useful relative to the alternatives already available to the buyer.

Mark how the chapter changes the comparison rather than trying to win every comparison at once.

03 / Separate recognition from meaning

Being known is not the same as being understood. Track the point where a familiar product gains a sharper reason to be chosen.

Ask what the product can credibly own and what it should leave to competitors.

04 / Run one position audit

Choose one product or service and write the association you want a buyer to retrieve first.

Then name the competing frame, the proof that supports your position, and the message you would remove to make the position clearer.

02 / The Principle

Narrow the promise until the choice becomes clear.

A strong position is not a complete description of a product. It is the most useful association the buyer can retrieve when alternatives compete for attention.

One example

A project-management tool may stop claiming to serve every team and instead own the position of the fastest way for a small creative team to see what is ready for review.

Deliberate practice - 15 minutes

Choose one product. Write the position it currently occupies, the competitor or category frame that dominates the comparison, and one narrower association the product can credibly own. Remove three supporting claims that do not strengthen that association.

03 / Field Notes

Four current signals about earning one clear position in a crowded decision path.

Anthropic / 2026-11-20

Anthropic's IPO turns model scale into a market position

What happened: Anthropic announced the closing of its initial public offering at $83 per share, with the stock beginning to trade on Nasdaq under ANT, alongside a large private placement involving major technology and semiconductor companies. Why it matters: The event makes a strategic association legible: Anthropic is no longer only a model maker but a publicly priced infrastructure and platform company. A position becomes stronger when the market can retrieve one coherent role from several pieces of evidence. Watch: Whether the financing and partner network sharpen Anthropic's distinct position, or leave it competing in the same undifferentiated frontier-model frame as everyone else.

Google / 2026-11-18

Google makes generated worlds easier to return to

What happened: Google rolled out Genie 4 in Project Genie to eligible Google AI Ultra subscribers, adding real-time natural-language editing, native audio, longer sessions, and a community gallery for sharing creations. Why it matters: The product is easier to place when the message moves from a generic claim about generation to a memorable job: make a world, change it, hear it, and revisit it. The gallery adds a social reason for the capability to become a habit. Watch: Whether longer sessions and sharing help Project Genie own a repeatable creative behavior rather than remain a technically impressive demo.

Marketing Dive / 2026-11-17

Holiday brands are using human-made detail as a felt difference

What happened: Marketing Dive reports that holiday marketers are balancing AI-enabled tactics with creator partnerships, deals, and human-made creative; Levi's used stop-motion claymation and E.l.f. used stop-motion in its holiday work. Why it matters: The differentiator is not merely that the work is handmade. It is that the production choice gives the brand one perceptible association—care, craft, or individuality—instead of asking the audience to remember a long list of campaign claims. Watch: Whether the visible craft strengthens durable brand association, or is remembered only as an executional novelty after the holiday campaign ends.

Retail Dive / 2026-11-17

Retailers are moving the holiday choice earlier and into AI comparison

What happened: Retail Dive reports that consumers began shopping early to manage budgets, while BCG found generative AI becoming a tool of choice for finding and comparing products, with more than half of consumers using it in 2026. Why it matters: The buying position is no longer formed only at the sale-day endpoint. Brands need one clear reason to enter the shopper's consideration set while the shopper is discovering, comparing, and managing uncertainty over time. Watch: Whether retailers build distinct messages for early planning, comparison, and last-minute urgency, instead of repeating the same discount frame across the whole season.

05 / The Practice

Make the right comparison easier to remember.

Do not ask a product to occupy every possible advantage. Give it one defensible association, make the comparison explicit, and let the rest of the message support that position.
