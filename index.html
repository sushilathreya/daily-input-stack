01 / The Canon

Eugene Schwartz: Find the hunger already in motion.

Primary assignment: Breakthrough Advertising, Chapter 1, “Mass Desire: The Force That Makes Advertising Work—and How to Focus It Onto Your Product.” Read the chapter itself first. The notes below are a source packet: passage markers, exact excerpts, and questions for the margin.

Field: human desire / persuasion fundamentalsReading: Chapter 1 from the opening through the State of Awareness sectionSource: local copy in Books

Source Packet

Do not skim this like a blog post. Open the local PDF, read the assigned chapter, then use these passages as anchors. The point is to see Schwartz's mental order: market first, desire second, headline late.

Passage 1 / Preface, market-making
“people don't change: only the direction of their desires do.”

Margin question: What desires are already visible in your market before any product is named?

Passage 2 / Preface, channeling
“All that is necessary is to be able to channel those wants into the proper products”

Margin question: Where is the audience already spending time, money, effort, shame, or attention to satisfy the desire badly?

Passage 3 / Chapter 1, headline's job
“Your headline has only one job—to stop your prospect and compel him to read the second sentence”

Margin question: Does your opening line try to sell the whole product, or does it create enough force for the next sentence?

Passage 4 / Chapter 1, new products
“Name the desire and/or its solution in your headline. Prove that that solution can be accomplished.”

Margin question: What must be named before the audience can recognize the offer as meant for them?

Passage 5 / Chapter 1, the writer's task
“To capture a feeling, a hope, a desire, a fear in words.”

Margin question: Which single feeling are you trying to focus? If there are three, choose one.

Close Reading Sequence

  1. Underline every place Schwartz separates desire from product.
  2. Mark the moment he moves from desire to awareness. That transition matters.
  3. List the jobs he gives to the headline. Notice what he refuses to ask the headline to do.
  4. Write one sentence in Schwartz's order: desire, recognition, proof, mechanism.

02 / The Principle

Don't manufacture hunger. Give it a direction.

Plainly: people rarely wake up wanting your category. They wake up wanting a better condition. The strategic question is which existing condition your offer can change in a way people can recognize and trust.

One example

Monzo's new platform does not lead with “better banking features.” It frames the benefit as money working quietly in the background so life can occupy the foreground. The underlying desire is not banking; it is agency without administrative burden. Features become proof of that larger promise.

Deliberate practice — 12 minutes

Pick one offer. Write three completions: “People are already trying to feel ______.” “They currently use ______ to get there.” “Our offer can credibly help because ______.” Then write one hook that starts with the first blank—not the product's name or feature.

@Hopeful_Rego / User-nominated

Category boundaries are moving on the digital shelf.

This is stronger because it is not a vibe. It reports actual category work: packaged fruit beverages are competing with coconut water, hydration, wellness drinks, cold-pressed juices, sparkling beverages, imports, fresh juice stalls, homemade beverages, and even appliances. That is Schwartz in the wild: the buyer's desire is not “juice.” It is hydration, health, convenience, taste, freshness, or ritual, and the competitive set follows the desire.

After spending the last 3 weeks researching India's packaged fruit beverage market, my 8,500-word deep dive is finally live.

Some of the most interesting findings:

• India's packaged fruit beverage market is no longer just about juice. Consumers searching for fruit juice now encounter coconut water, hydration products, wellness drinks, cold-pressed juices, sparkling beverages, nata de coco drinks and premium imports alongside conventional juices.

• Health and naturalness dominate brand messaging across nearly 300 SKUs.

• Product labelling is still adjusting after the June 2024 FSSAI guidance restricting “100%” claims on fruit juice labels.
