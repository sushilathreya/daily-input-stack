01 / Find the internal confusion

Miller treats unclear communication as an organizational cost, not merely a copy problem.

Mark the points where employees lose sight of the customer, the offer, or the next action.

02 / Turn the narrative into shared language

A BrandScript becomes operational when people across the company can use the same simple terms to explain what the organization does.

Notice which parts of the story are stable enough to guide different teams without becoming slogans detached from work.

03 / Connect clarity to behavior

The value of a clear story is measured in decisions, priorities, and customer-facing behavior.

Underline the moments where Miller links language to a concrete change in how people work.

04 / Audit one internal message

Choose a meeting brief, onboarding note, or team announcement that currently creates friction.

Rewrite its core message so the audience, problem, desired outcome, and next action are unmistakable.

02 / The Principle

A story earns its keep when it improves decisions.

The strongest brand narrative is usable language: it helps employees recognize the customer's problem, understand the promise, and choose the next helpful action.

One example

A support team does not receive a vague values poster. It gets a short decision rule that names the customer's problem, the promised outcome, and the response that moves the customer toward it.

Deliberate practice - 15 minutes

Take one internal message and write three lines: the customer problem, the promise the team must protect, and the next behavior the message should produce.

03 / Field Notes

Four current signals on making a company easier to understand, trust, and act on.

OpenAI / 2026-11-10

Businesses are being asked to maintain a machine-readable story

What happened: OpenAI introduced Businesses in ChatGPT, letting eligible companies claim a business profile, update details, add product feeds, see aggregated discovery insights, and build Business Agents. Why it matters: The brand story is becoming operational data: accuracy, availability, and a useful explanation now affect whether a company can be found and understood inside an answer engine. Watch: Audit whether your public product information can answer the questions a customer asks before they ever reach your site.

OpenAI / 2026-11-13

Agentic checkout makes trust part of the product promise

What happened: OpenAI introduced ChatGPT Wallet for limited U.S. rollout, with user approval and security checks for each purchase, tokenized payment details, and guidance for businesses to make sites and risk systems compatible with agents. Why it matters: A clear customer promise must now travel through discovery, comparison, payment, fulfillment, and support; a broken step in that chain makes the story feel false. Watch: Map the customer journey beyond the campaign: where could a legitimate buyer, agent, or support request be blocked by an internal system?

Marketing Dive / 2026-11-12

Unilever is tying social-first work to growth ownership

What happened: Unilever said its 2027 reorganization will use a global-strategy, local-execution model, while its marketing organization expands creator-led work and connects content, media, commerce, and growth responsibilities. Why it matters: The narrative is more useful when ownership is explicit: the promise is not just communicated by marketing but translated into decisions across teams and markets. Watch: For one initiative, name the promise, the local adaptation, and the person accountable for turning attention into customer value.

Meta Newsroom / 2026-11-12

Meta is using long-term demand to create a new supply story

What happened: Meta announced multi-year purchase commitments for lower-carbon cement, steel, electric freight, and recycled plastics, arguing that early demand can help suppliers reach commercial scale. Why it matters: A company story gains credibility when it explains how a stated goal changes procurement, supplier behavior, and the wider market—not only what the company says about itself. Watch: Look for the operating decision that makes a public commitment believable: budget, contract, process change, or measurable constraint.
