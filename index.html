The event changes everything.

Robert McKee's craft is not “add a story.” It is the more exacting question: what happens when a person's life is knocked out of balance—and what costly action must follow?

Chapter Reader

McKee's architecture is causal. Read each movement as an answer to the one before it: equilibrium, disruption, action, a more difficult world, an irreducible choice, consequence.

01 / The Gap

A story begins where expectation meets a result that does not arrive. A character acts because they expect a particular response from the world; the world responds differently. McKee calls the distance between expectation and result the gap. It is not a plot ornament. It is the opening of a problem, and the problem creates the need to act.

For a commercial story, that means you cannot start with your solution. Start with the old normal and the event that makes it impossible to continue as before.

02 / The Inciting Incident

The inciting incident radically upsets the balance of forces in the protagonist's life. It awakens a conscious or unconscious desire to restore balance. It must arrive early enough to put the whole work in motion; it must be specific enough that we can see what has been lost, threatened, or suddenly made possible.

Ask: what could happen that would make a competent person unable to proceed by routine?

03 / Progressive Complications

The first action meets a reaction the protagonist did not expect. The next action therefore has to be larger, more informed, and more exposed. McKee's progression is not repetition with louder music: complications must make the situation more difficult, move it in a new direction, and raise the stakes.

Each turn forces a revaluation: good to bad, secure to exposed, possible to impossible—or the reverse. Without revaluation, a sequence is activity, not story.

04 / Crisis

The crisis is the story's obligatory choice. It compresses the opposing values so that the protagonist must choose between irreconcilable alternatives. This is where the story's meaning becomes visible: not in what the character says they value, but in what they risk when there is no painless option.

Do not confuse crisis with noise. The test is whether the choice is both unavoidable and genuinely costly.

05 / Climax And Resolution

The climax is the action taken under that pressure; it produces the final and irreversible change in value. Resolution is the brief aftermath: the new balance of forces, seen clearly enough for the audience to feel what the action cost and changed.

McKee's sequence is therefore: disruption → desire → escalating attempts → irrevocable choice → changed reality. Cut any part of a brand story that does not earn the next arrow.

Close Reading Sequence

  1. Find McKee's distinction between a turning point and mere information.
  2. Mark every place he links desire to an action, rather than to an attitude.
  3. For each stage, write the value that changes: trust, control, status, safety, belonging.
  4. Read the crisis section twice. What would make its choice too easy?

02 / The Principle

Don't tell us the benefit. Disturb the old normal.

People follow a narrative because their prediction of the world fails. A good opening creates a precise mismatch; the rest earns the correction.

One example

A founder's “we built an AI tool” is a category statement. “Our support queue doubled after the launch; the team could answer faster but could no longer tell which customer was about to churn” is an inciting incident. The tool enters only after the lost balance is visible.

Deliberate practice — 15 minutes

Choose one offer. Draw five boxes: old normal; inciting incident; first attempt that fails; costly choice; new normal. Use facts from one customer conversation. Keep it to 120 words. If the choice costs nothing, rewrite the middle.

04 / Collected Fragments

Two observations worth carrying into the work.

@Hopeful_Rego / For You / 1 August 2026

Functional cola is a category with a longer memory than its current hype.

This earns its place because it pairs a specific product artifact with a category claim. Coca-Cola Plus gives the new “prebiotic soda” story some history: desire for cola has been re-routed through health, ingredients, and certification before. Useful question: which supposedly new category is really an old desire, given a fresh proof mechanism?

Long before today's prebiotic soda boom, Coca-Cola launched Coca-Cola Plus in Japan in 2017. A functional cola with added dietary fibre.

@rorysutherland / Following / 31 July 2026

The first diagnosis often controls the rest of the work.

Sutherland's “diagnostic momentum” is a sharp warning for marketers and product people. The initial frame can determine the options that get considered later. Before drafting the story, audit the diagnosis: is this really a retention problem, a trust problem, a status problem, or an onboarding problem?

There is a concept in medicine called “diagnostic momentum”. Whoever gets to the patient first will tend to diagnose them according to their own specialism, and then that first framing carries on down the line.
