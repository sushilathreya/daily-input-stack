01 / The climax is a value-changing choice

McKee treats the climax as the decisive event in which the story's central value is turned. Study what changes, not merely what happens.

Name the opposing values that the final choice forces into the open.

02 / Pressure makes the final turn necessary

A climax earns its force from the progressive complications that precede it. The final move should answer pressure that has been accumulating, not arrive as an unrelated display.

Trace which earlier choice or consequence removes the easy alternatives.

03 / Consequence reveals character

The decisive action exposes what the protagonist values when competing goods or losses can no longer be avoided. Action becomes meaning through its cost.

Separate a character's stated belief from the value their climax makes visible.

04 / Audit one ending

Choose one story, pitch, or product narrative. Write its central conflict, final choice, and irreversible consequence in three lines.

If the ending could be removed without changing the meaning of the whole, the narrative has not yet concentrated its values strongly enough.

02 / The Principle

Make the ending prove the central value.

A strong climax concentrates the story's pressure into a choice whose consequence changes the audience's understanding of what matters.

One example

A product story should not end with a feature list. It should show a customer making the difficult decision the product makes possible, then living with a concrete result.

Deliberate practice - 15 minutes

Choose one narrative. Write its central value conflict, decisive choice, and irreversible consequence. Remove any ending beat that does not sharpen one of those three elements.

One fragment from the timeline worth carrying into the work.

Leon Abboud / for_you / 2026-11-02

A startup wins when the new physics force a new story

This is worth keeping because it treats narrative as part of the operating system, not decoration. A strange technical or market bet has to become desirable before incumbents copy it, and the company has to keep moving fast enough that the story remains attached to real progress.

I just hung up from a 26 minute call that was supposed to be 90 seconds. An AI founder opted in on my funnel and I called to see if he was a fit. Two minutes in, he opened up. He had spent the last 12 months building four products: a GPU service, a social network, an AI scheduling tool and AI agents infra. Then he said this: "I've been building for so long but I have no users and I know nothing about marketing." I asked if he was doing any marketing. He said: "I'm not spending on marketing right now because I have no users." I stopped him there. "Is not having users a symptom or the root cause?" I asked him. "What do you mean?" he asked. "If you had started marketing six months ago, would you have users today?" "Potentially, yes." he answered So no users is the symptom. The root cause is no distribution. He was waiting for users before investing in marketing. But users come from marketing. He was pushing off the very thing that would lead his business growing. If you're a founder with something good that nobody uses, more features won't fix it. People knowing you exist will.
