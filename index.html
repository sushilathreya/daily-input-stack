01 / Find the mental handle

A corporate position begins with the short association a prospect can retain, repeat, and use when making a choice.

Notice how the chapter separates a company's full complexity from the one useful idea that can organize memory.

02 / Make the association credible

A position is stronger when the company's products, history, behavior, and proof all make the chosen association believable.

Mark the evidence that turns a slogan into a defensible place in the prospect's mind.

03 / Protect clarity as the company grows

Expansion can add capabilities while weakening the idea that made the company easy to classify.

Study which activities reinforce the position and which ask one name to carry too many unrelated meanings.

04 / Audit one company position

Choose one company and write the association it currently owns, the association it wants, and the strongest proof for the difference.

Then name one message, product, or behavior to remove or change because it makes the position harder to remember.

02 / The Principle

Make the company stand for one useful thing.

A corporate position gives complexity a memorable center. Choose the association that matters most to the prospect, then align proof and behavior so the name reinforces that idea wherever it appears.

One example

A workflow company may offer analytics, automation, and collaboration, but if buyers remember it as the easiest way to keep a distributed team aligned, its product story and proof should make that association unmistakable.

Deliberate practice - 15 minutes

Choose one company. Write the one association it should own, three pieces of proof that support it, and one current message or activity that makes the position less clear. Rewrite that message around the chosen association.

Five current signals on making a company easier to name, trust, and choose.

Anthropic / 2026-11-10

Anthropic turns its company story into a public-market proposition

What happened: Anthropic launched the roadshow for its proposed initial public offering of Class A common stock, following its October S-1 filing. Why it matters: A corporate position has to travel beyond product launches. The move asks investors and partners to remember Anthropic as a particular kind of company, so the company story, governance, and operating evidence now have to reinforce one legible association. Watch: Watch which association survives the roadshow: frontier-model capability, trusted enterprise infrastructure, or public-benefit governance—and whether the evidence makes that choice credible.

Google / 2026-11-10

Google positions Gemini Carbon around hard, long-horizon work

What happened: Google made Gemini 4 Carbon available to developers, enterprises, scientists, and consumers, emphasizing cybersecurity, long-horizon agentic workflows, complex software engineering, and scientific reasoning; it is offered at the same introductory price as Gemini 4 Argon. Why it matters: The naming and offer separate the model from the broader product family by giving it a job to own: tasks that need deeper reasoning and extended verification. A clear use-case association does more work than a general claim of being the strongest model. Watch: Watch whether users route genuinely difficult, multi-step work to Carbon while using Argon for ordinary tasks, or whether the distinction remains launch language rather than a durable buying heuristic.

Meta Newsroom / 2026-11-09

Meta makes teen protection part of Instagram’s product promise

What happened: Meta said it is expanding AI age-estimation technology globally to place millions more teens into Instagram Teen Account protections. Why it matters: The company is making a broad trust claim concrete through a recognizable behavior: age estimation triggers a defined protection experience. A corporate association becomes believable when the system changes what people encounter, not only what the company says about safety. Watch: Watch whether the protections are legible to teens and parents, and whether age-estimation errors or regional differences weaken the association Meta is trying to own.

Shopify Changelog / 2026-11-10

Shopify gives merchants a way to publish the facts agents use

What happened: Shopify’s changelog says merchants can configure self-serve returns and cancellations by market. Its adjacent Knowledge Base feature lets merchants review and publish the store facts AI agents receive, including return windows, shipping destinations, and gift services. Why it matters: In agentic commerce, a merchant’s position is partly represented by the facts an intermediary can retrieve. Shopify is making ‘the commerce foundation that agents can understand’ more tangible by putting control of those facts in the merchant’s hands. Watch: Watch whether merchants maintain these published facts as carefully as storefront copy, and whether accurate policies improve agent recommendations and reduce post-purchase friction.

Marketing Dive / 2026-11-05

Fresh Step uses AI to make speed part of a brand system

What happened: Fresh Step worked with agency RECE to move from brief to ads in about a month at one-tenth the cost, using AI as part of a response to category pressure. Why it matters: The useful position is not simply ‘we use AI.’ It is a repeatable operating promise: the brand can turn a market signal into relevant creative quickly. That association becomes stronger when the production system repeatedly supports the brand’s intended relevance. Watch: Watch whether faster production produces more distinctive, on-strategy work, or merely increases the volume of interchangeable ads.
